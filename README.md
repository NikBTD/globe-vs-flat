# Globe vs Flat Earth

**Two models, one sky.** A free simulator that shows what you would see from any place on Earth, at any moment, on the globe and on the flat Earth model, side by side. Compare both with the real sky outside your window and see which one fits.

▶ **Try it:** https://nikbtd.github.io/globe-vs-flat/

🎬 **Watch the demo video:**
- English: [Globe vs Flat Earth: Which One Matches the Real Sky?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U0Bl9ZEZHnk)
- Français : [Globe vs Terre Plate : lequel colle au vrai ciel ?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rFOXdjtQwbw)

Free, in English and French, works in any modern browser on a computer or tablet. No sign-up and no personal data collected.

## What you can do

- Pick any place on Earth (place list, town search, or latitude/longitude) and any date and time.
- See the sky and horizon each model predicts, side by side, with a table comparing each prediction to the real values (sunrise, Sun direction and height, horizon distance and dip).
- Climb from the ground to an airliner, the ISS or deep space.
- Real Sun, Moon, planets and stars, with Play, Follow and star trails.
- Real events: eclipses at their best viewing spots, the Final Experiment in Antarctica, the midnight Sun, and "we see too far" photos (Chicago, Toronto, the Alps from 440 km...).
- Distances between two places and real long-haul flights along their great-circle routes.
- Flat Earth settings (Sun and Moon height, spotlight size, firmament) to try to make the flat model work.

## How the two models are built

- **Globe:** the real Earth-Moon-Sun system, with positions computed for the chosen date and time. The Sun is the only light source, so day and night, Moon phases and eclipses come out on their own.
- **Flat Earth:** the Gleason map (North Pole in the centre, Antarctica as an ice wall around the edge), with a 51 km Sun and Moon circling 4,828 km above the disc, a "spotlight" Sun for night, and the stars on a firmament dome. The real Sun and Moon positions are drawn on top so the mismatch is visible.

## Privacy

The site is one self-contained page (`index.html`). It collects no data: no analytics, no cookies, no sign-in, no outside requests. Preferences (language, units, time zone, saved places) stay in the visitor's own browser.
